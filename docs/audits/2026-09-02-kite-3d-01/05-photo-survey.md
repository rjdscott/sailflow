# 05 — Photographic survey of the J/70 gennaker (evidence appendix)

14 J/70 photographs, collected 2026-09-02 by web search, measured by eye against
the visible mast: sheer at the mast = 0, masthead = 1, taken as 8.5 m. The
photographs are **not committed** to this repo (third-party copyright) and are
cited by image URL and page URL only. Two of them, rows A and M, were re-measured
independently by the orchestrator and agreed with the figures below within 0.03
of mast height.

This appendix is the reference the gennaker findings are measured against. It
records what the sail does on the water; it makes no claim about what the app
should draw beyond the comparison table at the end.

## Scale convention

All heights are fractions of the visible mast height: sheer or deck at the mast
= 0.00, masthead = 1.00. Metric conversion uses 8.5 m of mast above the sheer.

`m = fraction × 8.5`

## Method

1. Candidate images harvested by scraping (curl and grep) three sources that
   serve full-frame photos without auth: North Sails' J/70 blog CDN,
   Scuttlebutt's "PHOTOS: J/70 …" regatta galleries, and Flickr search result
   pages (`live.staticflickr.com`, `_b` = 1024 px long edge). Bing and Google
   image search and Alamy were blocked or returned junk.
2. Triaged with generated contact sheets, then each survivor was cropped to the
   target boat and overlaid with a labelled 5 %-of-frame grid before reading
   coordinates by eye. Estimates are quoted to the nearest 0.05, except where the
   corner was crisply identifiable (radial seams converging on the clew patch),
   where 0.01 to 0.02 resolution is claimed.
3. Class check. Every boat had to show the J/70 class insignia, or the
   single-spreader fractional rig plus retractable sprit plus open transom plus
   4 to 5 crew, and pass a proportion check: hull length divided by mast height
   should be about 0.815 (LOA 6.93 m / mast 8.5 m). Photos failing that were
   dropped, which caught several imposters (see Rejected).
4. Perspective error: the same hull/mast ratio graded each camera angle. A ratio
   of 0.80 to 0.82 means the camera is essentially abeam and vertical
   measurements are clean; 0.74 to 0.77 means about 10 % foreshortening; astern
   and bow-on views preserve vertical scale but make the clew corner much harder
   to identify, which is where most of the error lives.

## Summary statistics

**Clew height above the sheer, as a fraction of mast height** (14 readings from
13 photographs):

```
0.12  0.12  0.15  0.16  0.17  0.17  0.18  0.22  0.23  0.25  0.27  0.28  0.28  0.30
```

| population | n | median | range |
|---|---|---|---|
| all | 14 (incl. J2) | **0.19 (1.6 m)** | 0.12–0.30 (1.0–2.6 m) |
| **reach** (broad reach / planing, main out but not squared) | 8 | **0.20 (1.7 m)** | 0.15–0.30 (1.3–2.6 m) |
| **run** (deep, main squared, sail carried in front of the boat) | 6 | **0.16 (1.4 m)** | 0.12–0.28 (1.0–2.4 m) |

Caveat on the reach/run split: it is **not** significant. The run rows are
dominated by astern and bow-on views where the clew corner is the hardest thing
in the frame to pick out, and they carry the lowest confidence flags. The honest
statement is that the clew sits around 0.15 to 0.25 of mast height (1.3 to
2.1 m) above the sheer across every downwind mode measured, with a wider scatter
than any systematic reach/run difference.

**Tack height above the sheer at the mast** (9 readable):

```
0.01  0.02  0.02  0.03  0.03  0.04  0.04  0.04  0.05
```

Median **0.03 (≈0.25 m)**, range 0.01–0.05 (0.1–0.4 m). In every photo the tack
is pinned hard down on the sprit: it reads at or barely above the extended
sheerline. There is no meaningful tack-height variation between light and breeze
in this sample. The J/70 tack line is short and it is used short.

**Foot behaviour** (13 readable): sags below the tack-clew chord in 10, reads
straight in 3 (A, C, and marginally B), and **never lifts** in any photo.
Typical sag is 0.03 to 0.05 of mast height (0.25 to 0.4 m) below the chord; the
deepest measured (E, USA 347, light-medium air) is about 0.08 (0.7 m). The foot
is lowest at roughly one third of the way aft from the tack, not at mid-chord.

**Clew athwartships**, measurable only in the two dead-astern shots: about 2.0 m
(M) and 2.7 m (L) off the centreline, i.e. 0.9 to 1.6 m outside the rail given
the 2.25 m beam. Both are lower bounds, since the clew is forward of the transom
used for scale, so it is further from the camera and its apparent offset is
understated.

**Clew fore-aft**: in the six beam-on photos where it reads, the clew is
consistently abeam the mast to just aft of the shrouds, never near the transom.
The sheet does the travelling, running from there aft to a block on the transom
corner.

## Qualitative observations

1. **The whole sail floats low.** Head at the masthead, clew at 0.15 to 0.25 of
   mast height, tack at about 0.03, so the leech spans roughly the top three
   quarters of the rig and the last quarter is empty air above the deck. Nothing
   in this sample supports a clew above 0.30.
2. **The shoulders are high and well forward.** Maximum girth sits at roughly
   0.65 to 0.75 of the luff length up from the tack, and the widest part of the
   sail is projected *ahead of the forestay*, not abeam. On the beam-on shots the
   luff is typically 40 to 50 % of the hull length forward of the mast.
3. **The foot always hangs; it never lifts.** In 10 of 13 it bellies 0.25 to
   0.4 m below the tack-clew line, and the sag is asymmetric, deepest about a
   third aft of the tack, which is what a sail with a pinned tack and a
   free-flying clew should do.
4. **On a genuinely deep run the luff rotates to windward, but not far.** The one
   clean dead-astern run (M) puts the luff about 2 m to windward of the
   centreline, roughly one boat beam. The other run shots have the luff at or
   barely to windward of the mast line. This is nothing like a symmetric-kite
   projection: the J/70 sail stays largely on one side.
5. **The upper leech is open in every reaching photo and squarer on the runs.**
   Reaching, the top 20 % or so of the leech falls away to leeward and the head
   visibly twists off; running deep, the head squares up and in two shots (K, M)
   shows a slight hook to windward. No photo in this set shows a hooked leech
   while reaching.

## Comparison with the drawn kite

Read across: the tack and the foot are in the right country. The clew is the
outlier in both axes. Trimmed hard (sheet 100) the drawn clew is lower than any
photograph in the set, and fully eased it is at the top of the measured range,
so the app's sheet range spans nearly the whole population spread that 13 boats
in 5 to 22 kt produce. Athwartships, the drawn clew is 0.9 to 2.4 m further
outboard than either dead-astern photograph, at every sheet setting.
